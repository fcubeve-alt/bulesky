Everything written about heartbreak is about moving on. This isn't. Moving on is a question for a month from now. Tonight the only question is how to get from here to morning.

## Why nights are so much worse

It isn't your imagination. During the day you have tasks, noise, other people's faces — a hundred small things standing between you and the thought. At night all of it goes quiet at once, you're tired enough that your defences are down, and the thought gets the whole room to itself.

Add to that the fact that the night used to be theirs. The last message of the day, the call before sleep, the shape of someone next to you. The hours you're most alone are precisely the hours that used to be least alone. So the missing isn't a sign you're failing to cope. It's arriving exactly when it always would.

## Things that actually help at 3 a.m.

None of these fix anything. They're just what tends to work when it's dark and you're not going to sleep for a while.

- **Get out of the bed you're spiralling in.** Sit up, go to another room, put a light on. Lying still in the dark is the single most efficient way to think in circles.
- **Do something with your hands and nothing with your judgement.** Wash a dish, fold clothes, make tea you don't drink. Small physical tasks occupy the part of the mind that's been running the same argument for two hours.
- **Write the message you want to send — and don't send it.** Put it in your notes app, write it out completely, be as unfiltered as you like. Most of the urge is the need to say it, not the need for them to read it. Reread it tomorrow at noon, when you'll be a different person.
- **Cold water on your face and wrists.** It sounds like nothing. It genuinely takes the edge off a body that's stuck in alarm.
- **Tell someone, even a stranger.** Not to get advice. Just so the thing is outside of you, in words, being read by someone. It works even when it shouldn't.
- **Set the bar at getting to morning.** Not sleeping. Not being okay. Not "handling it well". Just morning.

## What not to do to yourself tonight

- **Don't check their profile.** You know this. You'll do it anyway. If you can, hand your phone to a friend or put the app in a folder you'd have to think to open. Every check restarts the clock.
- **Don't run the highlight reel.** At 3 a.m. memory only serves the best moments and the worst ones. Neither is the relationship you actually had.
- **Don't audit yourself.** Whatever the list of things you should have said or done differently, tonight is the worst possible time to write it. You are not conducting an honest review — you are looking for reasons to hurt.
- **Be careful with alcohol.** It shortens the evening and lengthens the 4 a.m.

## Missing them is not the same as needing them back

This is the part almost nobody is told. You can know with complete clarity that the relationship was wrong, that you ended it for good reasons, that you'd never go back — and still be flattened by missing them.

Missing someone is a habit of attention. For a long time, everything that happened to you was routed towards one person. That routing doesn't switch off because the reasoning changed; it wears down slowly, by not being used. Grief for a relationship isn't proof you made a mistake. It's proof it was real.

## What tomorrow actually looks like

Not linear. You'll have three decent days and then a song in a shop will take you apart. Recovery from heartbreak isn't a slope, it's a series of ambushes that get further apart. When one hits after a good week, it isn't a relapse. The distance between them is the real measurement.
