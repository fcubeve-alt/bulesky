It is easier to tell a stranger because a stranger cannot be disappointed in you
tomorrow.

Everyone you love is holding a version of you. The reliable one. The one who is
doing fine now. The one who got over it. Saying the true thing to them does not
just hand over information — it revises the person they think they know, and
some part of you is always calculating what that revision will cost.

A stranger is holding nothing. There is no version to protect. You can put the
whole thing down, and they will carry it for the length of a conversation, and
then you will both walk away.

## What you are actually avoiding

It is rarely the words. Most people can say "I am not okay" out loud. What stops
them is what happens in the four seconds afterwards.

Someone who loves you will *react*. Their face will do something. They will
worry, and then you will be managing their worry on top of your own. They may
try to fix it, because watching someone you love hurt and doing nothing is
almost unbearable — so they offer solutions you did not ask for, and now you
have to be grateful.

Or worse, they will remember. On Thursday they will ask how you are with a
particular tilt of the head, and you will realise you are now a person who is
being checked on.

None of that is their failure. It is simply the weight that comes attached to
being known.

## The confessional effect is real

Psychologists have a name for this — the stranger-on-a-train phenomenon — and it
turns up wherever there is closeness without consequence: night buses, hospital
waiting rooms, hairdressers, the smoking area outside a party, a taxi at 3am.

Three things have to be true at once. The person has to be present enough to
matter. They have to be leaving. And nothing you say can rearrange your life
tomorrow.

That combination is rare, which is why people can go months without saying the
true thing and then say all of it to someone whose name they never learn.

## It is not avoidance

There is a common worry underneath this: that telling a stranger is a way of not
dealing with it. Of getting the relief without the risk.

Mostly it is the opposite. Saying a thing out loud for the first time is how it
becomes sayable at all. A sentence you have only ever thought is shapeless — it
can mean anything, and at 3am it usually means the worst available thing. Said
out loud, even once, even to no one who matters, it becomes a fixed size. It
turns out to be a sentence rather than a weather system.

People very often tell someone who counts *after* they have told someone who
does not. The stranger was not instead of. It was the rehearsal.

## What this place is

That is most of what this site is for. You write the thing. It floats off. You
are not there when someone reads it, and they are not there when you wrote it,
and neither of you will ever know who the other is.

Sometimes someone answers. Not with advice, usually — with something closer to
*I read this and I am sorry and you are not the only one.* That turns out to be
the useful part far more often than any solution.

There is no account, no name, nothing to log back into. If you never come back,
nothing follows you.

## If it is more than you can carry

One honest limit: this works for the ordinary unbearable — grief, loneliness,
the end of things, the stuff there is no fix for. It is not a substitute for
help when you need help. If you are thinking about hurting yourself, please tell
someone who can actually do something tonight, not the internet.

For everything else — the sentence you have been carrying around for months, the
one that gets heavier the longer nobody has heard it — a stranger is a perfectly
good place to start.

Most people are more findable than they think. The thing keeping them silent is
usually not that no one would care. It is that everyone who cares would care too
much.
