You miss them because you are grieving a real relationship, and the fact that
parts of it were bad does not delete the parts that were not.

This is the thing almost nobody says plainly. People tell you to remember how
they treated you, as if you had forgotten. You have not forgotten. You can hold
the whole list — the things they said, the night you drove home shaking, the way
you learned to check their face before you spoke — and still find yourself
reaching for your phone at eleven at night. Both of those are happening in the
same person at the same time, and neither one is a mistake.

## Missing them is not a verdict on whether you should have left

The part of you that misses them is not conducting a review of the relationship.
It is not weighing evidence. It does not know that leaving was correct, because
that is not what it does.

It is the part that learned, over months or years, that this specific person was
where you went. Their name at the top of the screen. Knowing what they would say
about the thing that happened at work today. That knowledge does not get deleted
when the relationship ends, and it does not get deleted when you find out the
relationship was bad for you. It just sits there, still pointing at someone who
is no longer there.

So missing them proves that you attached to someone. It does not prove that you
should go back.

## Why the good memories are the loud ones

People expect the bad memories to protect them. Mostly they do not, and there
are ordinary reasons for that.

The good moments were the ones you were open during. You were not braced. You
were not managing anything. Those get stored differently from the ones you spent
defending yourself through, and they come back more easily and in more detail.

The bad moments, meanwhile, are exhausting to look at directly, so your mind
does what minds do and skips them — the same way you would not choose to rewatch
something that frightened you. What is left running, most evenings, is a
highlight reel that is not lying about anything and is not telling the truth
either.

And if the relationship was inconsistent — kind, then cruel, then kind again —
there is an extra thing happening. Intermittent kindness is far harder to let
go of than steady kindness, because you never got to finish learning what to
expect. The part of you that is still waiting is not being stupid. It is waiting
because waiting was, for a long time, sometimes rewarded.

## The question that actually helps

Not "was it really that bad?" You will lose that argument at 2am no matter what
happened, because at 2am the person you are missing is a feeling, not a person.

A better one: **what was I getting, and where else could it come from?**

Sometimes the answer is company. Sometimes it is being known without explaining
yourself. Sometimes it is much smaller and more embarrassing — someone to tell
about a parking ticket. That is not a trivial thing to lose. Naming it makes it
findable somewhere else. "I miss them" is not findable anywhere.

## What helps, in the order it usually helps

**Stop trying to stop missing them.** Fighting it makes it the only thing in the
room. Missing someone is not the same as being about to contact them, and
treating them as the same thing is what turns a quiet evening into a crisis.

**Put the reaching somewhere.** The urge to tell them something is a real urge
with a real target. Write the message and do not send it. Say it out loud to an
empty room. Put it somewhere anonymous. It sounds like a substitute because it
is one — but an unsent sentence is finished, and an unsaid one runs all night.

**Do not audit yourself for still caring.** "I should be over this by now" adds
a second problem on top of the first. There is no schedule. People who left far
worse relationships than yours still miss them on Sunday afternoons.

**Make contact a decision, not a moment.** If you are going to reply, decide it
in daylight, with the whole picture in front of you. The version of you at
midnight is not being given a fair vote.

## If it was more than hurt

If you were frightened of them, if you were controlled, followed, threatened, or
made to account for every hour — missing them is still normal, and it is also
not the main fact. That is not something to reason your way through alone. A
domestic abuse line will talk to you even if you are not sure it counts, even if
you have left, even if you want to go back. Not being sure is the most common
reason people call.

## One more thing

You are not being weak, and you have not undone anything by feeling this. The
missing is not evidence for their case. It is only evidence that you were
somebody who let another person matter — which is the same capacity you are
going to need for whoever comes next, and it is worth not being ashamed of.

Some nights it just needs saying somewhere, to nobody in particular, without
anyone telling you what you already know about them. That is what this place is
for.
