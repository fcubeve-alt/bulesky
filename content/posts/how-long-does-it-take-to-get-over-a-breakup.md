There is no number, and the numbers you have been given are part of why this
feels like it is going badly.

Half the length of the relationship. Three months. A year for every five.
Someone always has a formula, delivered with confidence, and every one of them
is invented. What they really do is hand you a deadline — and then, when you are
still bad on the day it expires, they hand you a second problem: that you are
behind.

You are not behind. There is no schedule to be behind on.

## What actually changes

Getting over someone is not one process. It is several, and they finish at
completely different times, which is why it feels so incoherent from the inside.

**The habits go first.** Reaching for your phone at the time you always messaged.
Buying the thing they like at the shop. Turning to say something in a room where
nobody is standing. These fade with repetition and they fade reasonably fast —
weeks, usually. This is the part people mean when they say you are doing better.

**The reflex takes longer.** Something good happens and they are still the first
person you want to tell. This can go on well past the point where you have
stopped wanting them back, which is disorienting enough that people assume it
means they are not over it. It does not.

**The story is last.** What that relationship was, what it meant, what it says
about you. This one is not on a timer at all. It rearranges itself for years,
usually quietly, usually without you noticing until you catch yourself
describing it differently to someone and realise the account has changed.

You can be completely finished with the first and nowhere near the third. That
is not stalling. That is the normal shape of it.

## Why it is not a straight line

Nobody warns you about month five.

The usual pattern is not a slope downwards. It is a rough decline with sudden
returns to somewhere near the start, triggered by things that make no sense — a
song, obviously, but also a smell, a specific quality of light in October, a
stranger's laugh in a corridor.

Those returns are not a reset. You have not lost the ground. Memory is
associative, and the associations do not decay evenly; some of them just take
much longer to come up in the shuffle. The fifth bad day is not the same as the
first bad day, even when it feels identical.

## The thing that genuinely slows it down

Not the crying. Not the missing them. Those are the process running.

What actually extends it is **contact that keeps the question open**. Checking
their profile. The occasional friendly message. Staying in a version of the
friendship where reconciliation remains technically possible.

This is not a moral point about willpower. It is mechanical: you cannot finish
grieving something that has not finished. Every check restarts the clock,
because every check is your mind receiving new information about a live
situation rather than old information about a closed one.

Most people know this. Most people do it anyway for a while. That is also
normal, and it is worth knowing what it costs rather than pretending it is free.

## What "over it" turns out to mean

Not that you never think about them. Not that you feel nothing. People wait for
an indifference that mostly never arrives, and conclude they have failed.

What actually happens is smaller. One day the thought arrives and it is just a
thought. It has weight but it does not take the whole day. You can hold it and
also make dinner. You can hear their name and something moves in your chest and
you carry on with the conversation.

That is it. That is the whole thing. It is much quieter than people expect and
it usually gets noticed in retrospect.

## If it has stopped being grief

There is a line worth naming. Grief hurts and moves. If weeks are going by with
no eating, no sleeping, nothing mattering, or if you have started thinking the
world would be simpler without you in it — that is not a slow recovery, that is
something else, and it responds to help. Tell a doctor, or somebody who loves
you. Plainly. Today.

## Meanwhile

If you want a number, here is the only honest one: longer than you want, and
less time than it feels like tonight.

And in the meantime you do not have to be quiet about it. Saying it somewhere —
even into the dark, even to nobody in particular — is one of the few things that
reliably makes the next hour easier.
