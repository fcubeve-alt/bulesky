You do not have to stop loving them to let go. That is the part almost everyone
gets wrong, and it is why this drags on for years.

Most people are waiting for a feeling to arrive — some day when the love has
drained out and they can finally move. So they check. Still love them? Then it
is not time yet. And they wait, and the waiting itself keeps everything exactly
where it is.

Letting go is not an emotion that shows up. It is a set of things you stop
doing, while the feeling is still fully present.

## What is actually being held

Usually not the person. Usually the door.

The love is not the problem — you can love someone for the rest of your life
without it costing you anything. What costs is the small live possibility you
are keeping open: that they might message, that this might not be final, that
some version of this could still work out.

You can tell which one you are carrying by what you do when the phone lights up.
If there is a jolt, that is the door, not the love.

## The reason it will not close on its own

Grief needs the thing to be over before it can finish. It is not being stubborn;
it simply cannot process a situation that is still receiving updates.

So every check of their profile, every "just being friendly" message, every
mutual friend telling you what they are up to, hands your mind new information
about a live situation. And your mind, quite reasonably, keeps the file open.

That is why people who cut contact often describe it as brutal for two weeks
and then noticeably lighter — and why people who stay in touch describe month
eleven as feeling much like month two. Nothing is wrong with the second group.
They are simply asking themselves to finish something that has not finished.

## What letting go actually is

It is a series of small, dull decisions, none of which feel like closure:

Not answering immediately. Then not answering the same day. Then, eventually,
not needing to decide.

Taking the photos off the phone's front screen, and not deleting them, because
deleting is a different act and you do not owe anyone that.

Telling the mutual friend, once, that you would rather not hear updates.

Letting a birthday pass without a message, which will feel like a betrayal and
is not one.

None of these produce a feeling of release. They just make the space where the
feeling is gradually smaller, until one day it is a thing you carry rather than
a thing you are inside.

## The two-year mistake

There is a common trap: staying friends immediately, because you cannot bear the
total absence.

Sometimes this genuinely works. More often it converts a clean loss into a
long one — you get a fraction of the person, which is enough to keep hoping and
nowhere near enough to be alright, and you spend two years in a state that
neither ends nor becomes anything.

Friendship after love is real and possible. It usually requires having actually
let go first, not instead.

## What it looks like when it happens

Not indifference. Almost nobody gets indifference, and expecting it is another
way of concluding you have failed.

What arrives instead: you think of them and it is a thought rather than an
event. You hear the song and it is a nice song. You find out they are seeing
someone and something turns over in your chest, and then you get on with your
afternoon.

You will probably not notice this happening. People usually notice it
afterwards, catching themselves not having checked.

## Tonight

You do not have to stop loving them. You are allowed to love them for years.

You only have to stop waiting for the door to be used.

And if that sentence is a hard one to sit with alone at this hour — it is worth
saying out loud somewhere, even to no one who knows your name. It gets slightly
lighter for having been said.
