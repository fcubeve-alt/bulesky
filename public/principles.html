Community Principles

Pain is welcome. Harm is not.
That's the whole rule. Everything below is just what it means in practice.

This is a healing space, not a free-speech arena

Plenty of the internet exists for arguing. This isn't that. People come here at two in the morning holding something they can't say anywhere else, and the only thing that makes it possible is knowing what they'll get back. So we protect the tone deliberately, and we'd rather lose a user than lose the safety.

What is always welcome

Anything you feel about your own life, however dark it looks written down:

  • missing someone who is gone, or who left;
  • heartbreak, loneliness, the kind of night that doesn't seem to end;
  • regret, shame, guilt, the thing you never got to say;
  • exhaustion, numbness, feeling like a burden;
  • fear about the future, and hope for it too.

None of that is "negativity" to be cleaned up. That's a person telling the truth, which is the hardest thing anyone does here.

What is never allowed

The line is simple: expressing your own pain, yes. Aiming something at another person, no.

  1. Attacks on a particular person — insults, curses, exposing who someone is, calling for others to go after them. Whether they are a user here or somebody named in a whisper.
  2. Mocking or belittling someone's pain — "get over it", "that's pathetic", "other people have it far worse", laughing at what someone just risked saying.
  3. Teaching or encouraging self-harm — describing a method, urging someone to end their life, explaining how it is done. Writing that you feel this way about yourself is allowed; telling someone else how to act on it is not.
  4. Threats and violence — against anyone, including violent fantasies about a named person.
  5. Discrimination based on sex, sexuality, race, nationality, religion, disability, appearance, or class.
  6. Advertising, recruiting, asking for contact details — promotion of any kind, pulling people into private chats, leaving a WeChat, an email or a social handle. Contact details are masked automatically.
  7. Sexual content that is explicit rather than a feeling. Anything sexual involving a minor: zero tolerance, removed on the first report.
  8. Spam — meaningless repetition, gibberish, walls of emoji.

Bullying is the one to name twice, because it can be done without a single banned word: following one person from balloon to balloon, answering everything they write. That is why one device can leave at most three replies on the same whisper.

How to reply well

You don't have to fix anything. Nobody here expects advice, and most of the time advice isn't what helps.

  • Say what you'd say if this were your friend, sitting next to you.
  • "I read this. You're not alone tonight" is enough. It is often the best reply on the whole page.
  • Don't start with "at least…". Don't compare their pain to yours to shrink it.
  • If you don't know what to say, leave a light instead of typing. It's a real answer.
One sincere sentence can keep a stranger company for a very long time.

Kindness Guard

We call the protection Kindness Guard rather than "moderation", because its job isn't to police speech — it's to keep this one quality of air. It works in layers:

  • Filters. Contact details — email, phone numbers, messaging handles — are masked before anything is saved, and a list of outright slurs is refused outright.
  • Something that actually reads it. Every whisper and every reply is read by a moderation model as it is written, in any language. A keyword list only ever covered Chinese and English; this is what covers the rest.
  • It fails in your favour, on purpose. The only thing refused outright is content that could get someone hurt: a method written out in enough detail to follow, a threat against a real person, sexual content involving a child. Nothing you write about your own pain can reach that line, however dark it is — and if the model is down or unsure, your whisper goes up. We would far rather review an attack afterwards than refuse someone at three in the morning because a machine misread grief.
  • Anything else it flags still goes up — and straight into a queue to be looked at, rather than waiting for a stranger to be hurt by it first.
  • Reporting. Every whisper and every reply has its own report button. Each report runs the same check: a confirmed violation is hidden straight away. Independently of that, three reports hide it regardless — so a wrong verdict, or no verdict at all, can't keep it up.
  • Replies are short and few. 150 characters, and at most three replies from one device on the same whisper. Length is what a long put-down needs and what a short kindness doesn't; the limit on repeats is there because following someone down a thread is its own kind of harm.
  • A hotline that's always there. The crisis numbers sit above the box while you're writing a sorrow — not triggered by what you type, so nobody has to be flagged by a machine to be offered help.
  • Hidden means hidden. Once content is hidden it's gone for everyone, including whoever wrote it. Looking your own name up still shows that it existed, and that it was removed.
  • Nothing stays forever. Whispers and their replies are deleted a year after they were written — really deleted, row and words, not hidden. A sentence written at three in the morning two years ago is not the same object as the person who wrote it.

What we do, and what we can't

What we do

When you post something that matches crisis-related keywords, we'll show you a support hotline. When content or a reply is reported, we use AI to automatically judge whether it violates our guidelines; confirmed violations or reports that reach a certain count are automatically hidden. This is what we're able to do.

What we can't do

Are you all right? is fully anonymous — we don't know who you are, can't verify anyone's identity, and have no way to contact any user. We don't manually review content post by post; handling is automatic and doesn't mean a person is watching or will reach out.

Your responsibility

You're responsible for what you write and post here. If you're in real crisis, please reach out to a real support line — the hotline numbers on this page are a real course of action, this platform is not. Harmful language toward others will be handled; content that violates community guidelines will be hidden.

Disclaimer

Are you all right? does not provide counseling, medical, or legal services. We do our best to keep this a safe and warm space, but we do not take on responsibility for reviewing or moderating user-generated content.

这里欢迎什么

任何属于你自己的感受，写下来多难看都可以：思念、心碎、孤独、后悔、羞耻、愧疚、疲惫、麻木、恐惧，也包括希望。 对已经离开的人说的话，那些当时没来得及说出口的话。对自己的失望，对明天的不确定。 任何一句「我现在很难受，但不知道跟谁说」。

这些不是需要被清理的「负能量」，是一个人在说实话 —— 在这里这是最难的一件事。

这里不允许什么

界线很简单：写你自己的事，可以；把它对准另一个人，不行。

  1. 针对具体某个人的攻击 —— 辱骂、诅咒、人肉、公开别人的隐私、号召大家去攻击谁。
  2. 嘲讽或贬低别人的痛苦 —— 「矫情」「想开点就好了」「别人比你惨多了」，笑话一个刚刚鼓起勇气说出口的人。
  3. 教人或鼓励自我伤害 —— 描述具体方法、劝人结束生命、讲怎么做。写你自己有这种念头是允许的；告诉别人怎么去做，不行。
  4. 威胁与暴力 —— 对任何人的人身威胁，包括对某个具体的人的暴力幻想。
  5. 歧视 —— 基于性别、性取向、种族、国籍、宗教、残疾、外貌、阶层的攻击。
  6. 广告、拉人、索要联系方式 —— 任何形式的推广、招募，把人拉去私聊，留微信 / 邮箱 / 社交账号。联系方式会被自动屏蔽。
  7. 色情与露骨性内容（以性为主要目的的描写，不是情绪表达）。涉及未成年人的任何内容：零容忍，第一次举报就下架。
  8. 垃圾与刷屏 —— 无意义重复、乱码、纯表情刷屏。

「跟踪式回复」要单独说一次，因为它可以一个脏字都不带：跟着一个人从这个气球到那个气球，他写什么你回什么。 所以同一台设备对同一条最多只能回三次。

我们做了什么

每一条悄悄话和每一条回复，在写下来的那一刻就会被读一遍（任何语言）。能直接拦下的只有一类：可能让人真的受伤的内容 —— 写得出可照做的自伤方法、对真人的威胁、涉及未成年人的性内容。你写自己的痛苦，无论多重，都够不到这条线； 如果判断系统坏掉或者拿不准，你的悄悄话照发。其余判定为不妥的内容会正常发布，但立刻进入待查队列， 而不是等到有人被伤到才有人看。命中危机关键词时会显示心理援助热线。被举报的内容走同一套判断， 确认违规立即隐藏；独立于此，三次举报无论如何都会隐藏。内容满一年会被真正删除，不是隐藏。

我们做不到什么

这是一个完全匿名的产品——我们不知道你是谁，无法核实任何人的身份，也没有办法联系到任何一个用户。我们不会逐条人工审核内容，处理机制是自动的，不代表有人会看到、会联系你或对方。

你的责任

你在这里写下的、回复的每一句话，由你自己负责。如果你正在经历真实的危机，请联系专业的帮助渠道——页面上的热线电话是真实可用的行动方案，而不是这个平台。伤害他人的语言（辱骂、攻击性内容）会被处理，违反社区准则的内容会被隐藏。

免责

本站不提供心理咨询、医疗或法律服务。我们尽力提供一个安全、温暖的倾诉空间，但对用户发布或回复的内容不承担审核和监管责任。

If you're the one in danger

If you're thinking about ending your life, please talk to someone who can help tonight. In mainland China, the national mental-health hotline is 12356, 24 hours. In the US and Canada, call or text 988. In the UK and Ireland, call 116 123 (Samaritans). Elsewhere, findahelpline.com lists a number for most countries.

This sky is full of strangers who care, but none of us can reach you the way those people can.

Why we're strict about this

A place where nobody is ever attacked is much harder to build than a place with lots of users. It's the only thing here that can't be copied, and it exists entirely because of how you answer the next person.
